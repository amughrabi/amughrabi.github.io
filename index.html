Ahmad AlMughrabi

PhD candidate in Computer Vision · Universitat de Barcelona

I am a PhD candidate at the Faculty of Mathematics and Computer Science, Universitat de Barcelona, and a member of the AIBA research group. My thesis applies neural radiance fields and 3D reconstruction to realistic, accurate volume estimation of objects, with food and dietary assessment as the driving application. From October 2025 to January 2026 I was a visiting researcher with Benjamin Busam at the Technical University of Munich.

Before the PhD I spent more than ten years as a software engineer (Atypon, Glovo), working in Java, Python, front-end and DevOps. I hold an Honours MSc in Computer Science from the University of Jordan (2020) and an Honours BSc in Computer Science from Zarqa University (2012).

Publications

OneVol: When Does Geometry Add to Recognition? A Fusion Benchmark for Monocular Volume Estimation

Ahmad AlMughrabi, Farid Al-Areqi, Umair Haroon, Shervin Naderi, Hyunjun Jung, Martin Kampel, Benjamin Busam, Ricardo Marques, Petia Radeva

2026  project page / code

A fusion benchmark that factors a monocular volume estimate into six channels and combines them on eight datasets. The median volume of same-class training objects, using no image geometry at all, beats the full monocular pipeline in 23 of 24 dataset–backbone cells; geometry only adds when it is fused on recognition's terms.

MomentAux: When Does Fusing Hand-crafted Knowledge with Learned Representations Pay?

Ahmad AlMughrabi, Benjamin Busam, Ricardo Marques, Petia Radeva

arXiv 2026  project page / arXiv / code

A cost-normalized benchmark of stacking, substitution and interference: one hand-crafted spectral prior against data-driven alternatives under a single frozen recipe, across 14 datasets and 11 backbones. The free prior is worth more to a small transformer than two rounds of self-supervised pre-training, and nothing to a convolutional network that already has enough data.

BenchSeg: A Large-Scale Dataset and Benchmark for Multi-View Food Video Segmentation

Ahmad AlMughrabi, Guillermo Rivo, Carlos Jiménez-Farfán, Umair Haroon, Farid Al-Areqi, Hyunjun Jung, Benjamin Busam, Ricardo Marques, Petia Radeva

arXiv 2026  project page / arXiv / code

A multi-view food video segmentation dataset and benchmark: 55 dish scenes with 25,284 annotated frames under free 360° camera motion, and 20 state-of-the-art segmentation models evaluated alone and combined with video-memory modules.

VolETA: One- and Few-shot Food Volume Estimation

Ahmad AlMughrabi, Umair Haroon, Ricardo Marques*, Petia Radeva*

CVPR 2024 MetaFood Workshop Challenge winner  project page / arXiv / code

VolETA estimates food volume from one or a few RGBD images by reconstructing a scaled 3D mesh of the food object, reaching 10.97% MAPE on the MTF dataset.

FoodMem: Near Real-time and Precise Food Video Segmentation

Ahmad AlMughrabi, Adrián Galán, Ricardo Marques, Petia Radeva

arXiv 2024  project page / arXiv

FoodMem segments food items from video sequences of 360-degree unbounded scenes. It generates consistent masks of food portions across a video, overcoming the flickering and prohibitive inference speeds of existing semantic segmentation models.

MomentsNeRF: Leveraging Orthogonal Moments for Few-Shot Neural Rendering

Ahmad AlMughrabi, Ricardo Marques, Petia Radeva

arXiv 2024  project page / arXiv

Orthogonal moments inside convolutional networks for one- and few-shot neural rendering: fewer artefacts, lower learning complexity and less noise than earlier few-shot NeRFs.

Pre-NeRF 360: Enriching Unbounded Appearances for Neural Radiance Fields

Ahmad AlMughrabi, Umair Haroon, Ricardo Marques*, Petia Radeva*

arXiv 2023  project page / arXiv

Overcomes several obstacles of earlier NeRFs on real 360° captures: handling multiple video inputs, selecting keyframes, and extracting poses from ambiguous, symmetrical real-world frames.
