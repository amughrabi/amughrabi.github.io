When does fusing hand-crafted knowledge with learned representations pay?

A controlled, cost-normalized benchmark and a measurable rule for stacking, substitution and interference

Abstract

Fusing prior knowledge with data-driven learning is attractive precisely where data is scarce, yet no controlled account says when such fusion helps, when it is redundant, and when it harms. We present a benchmark that measures one fixed, hand-crafted source of knowledge — a pinned bank of Gabor targets injected only during training at ~2% compute overhead — against the data-driven alternatives (SimCLR, SimSiam and DINO pre-training, ImageNet transfer, heavy augmentation, learned teachers) under one frozen recipe with fixed subsets: 13 datasets, 9 backbones, 500 to 1.28M images, 32 to 224 px, and 5.7M to 86M parameters (2,894 configurations over 8,827 runs).

Every pairwise combination is measured too, and all follow one rule: different-currency sources stack, same-currency sources substitute, and fusing into an already-informed initialization interferes in proportion to what it carries. Because each source's currency is measurable on frozen features beforehand, the outcome is predictable in advance, which we state as a procedure. Underlying it is an empirical decomposition, Δ = G + readout(base), whose sign holds on 79% of testable cells and which, registered in advance, called an unseen backbone family's feature gain from baseline heights alone.

The three outcomes

Combining two sources of knowledge produces one of three outcomes, and which one you get is not predictable from the sources' family names — the same prior is redundant with effective self-supervision and complementary with augmentation. It is predictable from a cheap measurement on frozen features, taken before the combination is built.

Stack — different currencies

The prior injects oriented-energy structure; heavy augmentation teaches invariance to nuisance transforms. Different currencies, so they compound. On ViT-tiny at 10% of CIFAR-100, the prior adds +13.3 under the plain recipe and +21.0 on top of DeiT augmentation.

Substitute — same currency

The prior and contrastive pre-training fill the same feature deficit, so combining them adds nothing over the better single source. Measured on the feature side: whichever source has the larger frozen-feature gain is the one that wins end-to-end, at 6 cells out of 6 across two datasets.

Tax — fusing into an already-informed initialization

Injecting the prior on top of ImageNet weights is destructive everywhere, and the damage is proportional to what the initialization was contributing: up to −17 points on photographic data, and statistically zero on histopathology where those weights were worth little to begin with. Probing confirms the loss is feature-side, not a classifier failure.

The rule

Registered in advance. Each experimental wave was launched with numeric prediction bands and explicit falsifiers recorded before any result existed, and outcomes are reported against those bands including the misses. In one case the rule was used to predict an unseen backbone family's feature gain from its baseline accuracies alone; all four cells landed inside the band. In others our predictions were wrong, and the paper scores them that way.

A procedure for deciding whether to fuse

The practical content of the taxonomy is that the outcome can be estimated before the combined system is built, from measurements of each source alone. The only cost beyond training each source once is two linear evaluations on frozen features, which run in minutes on cells that took hours to train.

  1. Train the baseline, source A alone and source B alone; record each arm's gain and its declared compute multiple.
  2. Evaluate all three frozen representations under an identical linear protocol. This gives GA and GB.
  3. If either source's gain is about zero, it supplies nothing to overlap, and the combination is not the question.
  4. If the two feature gains are close and both arms improve the same cells, expect substitution: take the cheaper source.
  5. If they differ in kind, expect stacking.
  6. If one source is a mature initialization, do not inject a shaping prior on top: expect a tax proportional to what that initialization supplies, measurable in advance as its advantage over training from scratch.

The benchmark

Scale2,894 configurations over 8,827 training runs
Datasets13, spanning natural images, satellite, texture, fine-grained, food and histopathology, plus multi-sensor Sentinel-1/2 pairs
Backbones9 across 5 families: ResNet-18/34/50, ConvNeXt, MobileNetV3, Swin, ViT-tiny/S/B
Data scale500 to 1.28M images; 32 to 224 px; 5.7M to 86M parameters
ComparatorsSimCLR, SimSiam, DINO, ImageNet transfer, DeiT augmentation, learned teachers — each at a declared multiple of baseline training cost
ProtocolOne frozen recipe; fixed per-class subset indices shared by every run; numerically pinned filter banks; every table regenerated by command
What we disclose. Configuration selection used the test split on CIFAR-100, which we mark as a selection set whose absolute values carry a bias of unknown size; the chosen setting was then applied verbatim to twelve further datasets that played no part in selection. Comparator tuning parity is asymmetric by construction under cost normalization, so our self-supervised numbers are lower bounds — and we measured how far below by re-running them at four times their budget. Two accuracy claims of ours did not survive that test and were withdrawn.
